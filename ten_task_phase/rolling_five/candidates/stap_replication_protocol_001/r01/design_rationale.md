# Design rationale

A replication-evidence audit is meaningful even when the original claim is retracted: it asks what later papers actually attempted and which kinds of evidence their conclusions rest on. Its scope is a finite observed indexed open-access citing cohort, not a claim to enumerate every public account of STAP.

The four-column table is sufficient to preserve procedure and marker/function distinctions while avoiding experimental denominators and exhaustive technical transcription. Eligibility includes early-stage failures and modified protocols, preventing a success-only or perfect-protocol selection bias. It excludes chronic acid marker research because the research target is transient induction, not because its marker result is positive.

The public source description identifies the European life-science publication/full-text index by function and geography without its site name or URL. The seed is identified by the scientific claim, journal/month and companion/retraction distinction. The citing/OA registration scope is explicit; omitting that scope would create a different literature-completeness question.

Source identity drives the reverse-citation state, and discovered article identities drive main-text access. The complete core packet demonstrably lacks required Niwa and Aizawa fields. No additional dependency layer is required or claimed. Difficulty remains only a hypothesis until the unchanged protocol is run in isolation.
